# Business Model Canvas
## Customer Segments

- Students doing dual degrees


## Value Propositions

- Ability to select the desired courses, and see the impact that has on other courses (e.g. some others are no longer options, or some have/are prerequisites)
- Provide information on the assessment load for each course/semester to help decide the combination of courses for each semester
- Filter the course display by degree
- Allow students to create a plan, and an easier way to manage a major/minor change


## Channels

- A specifically-made website


## Customer Relationships

- Reviews for courses


## Revenue Streams

- Long-term trust in the university due to the reliable service; therefore increase in enrollments


## Key Resoucres

- UQ course list and requirements
- Assessment information for each course


## Key Activities

- Retrieve data on the courses
- Create the website, with aforementioned functionality
- Refine the UI


## Accepted Hypotheses

Matching students with a mentor who did the same dual-degree combo will increase confidence more than generic advice.
A “what if I change major/minor?” simulator will help students explore options without fear of breaking their plan.
Elective suggestions based on preferences (e.g., hates group work, avoids finals) will make choosing between similar options easier.
Offering automatic alternatives when a course is full (summer option, swap, or path change) will keep students on track.
Offering backup options (e.g., if a course is full, suggesting an equivalent or summer course) will keep students on track.
Showing students a visual timeline of both degrees side by side will reduce confusion compared to just a list of courses.
Providing a space to ask questions to past or current students (like a moderated forum or chat) will reduce reliance on trial-and-error planning.
Incorporating peer ratings or student reviews for electives will increase trust in course suggestions compared to system-generated recommendations alone.
Integrating “add alternative” planning options in the degree planner will improve students’ sense of control over their academic timeline.
Showing visual progress comparisons (current path vs new path) will help students make more informed and confident decisions.


## Rejected Hypotheses

Short case studies from past dual-degree students (e.g., “How I managed my workload” or “Why I chose summer courses”) will help new students feel less overwhelmed.
Offering mentor-matching based on shared interests (not just degree combination) will make students feel more personally connected and supported.
