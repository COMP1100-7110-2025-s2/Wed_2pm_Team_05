# Test Cards For MVP
## Hypothesis 1: Students will be more on track if they are offered backup options for their degrees. 
**HYPOTHESIS**
 We believe that offering backup options (e.g., if a course is full, suggesting an equivalent or summer course) will keep students on track.

**TEST**
 To verify that, we will conduct interviews with students and ask them their input on being offered backup or alternative options.

**METRIC**
 And measure the level of usefulness in keeping the students on track/ schedule.

**CRITERIA**
 We are right if most of the students report that they will feel much more on track if they are offered backup options like alternative courses.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1.	If you have taken a dual degree, have you seen your planner?  What do you like about it? What didn't you like about it?
2.	Would you find it useful if our website offered alternative courses to take if the course you were going to take ends up full or for some other reason you can no longer take it?
3.	Would you find a custom plan for your entire degree useful? Would you use it?
4.	How useful will it be to keep you on schedule if you use it?

## Hypothesis 2: Displaying a visual timeline of degrees side by side would be much clearer to students compared to a simple course list.

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that showing students a visual timeline of both degrees side by side will reduce confusion compared to just a list of courses.

**TEST**
To verify that, we will interview students and ask them whether a visual timeline will be less confusing compared to a course list.

**METRIC**
And measure how much less confusing it will be for them if they were shown a visual timeline instead.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if most students report that they would think it will be much less confusing if they were shown a visual timeline of their degrees instead of a list of courses (based on their preferences).

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1.	Do you think being able to plan your entire degree out in a visual timeline will reduce confusion as compared to just the current list of courses?
2.	How much less confusing do you think it will be for you?

## Hypothesis 3: Dual-degree students will be much less overwhelmed if they have access to case studies from past students with the same dual degrees.

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that short case studies from past dual-degree students (e.g., “How I managed my workload” or “Why I chose summer courses”) will help new students feel less overwhelmed.

**TEST**
To verify that, we will conduct interviews with dual-degree students and ask them whether case studies from previous students will prevent them from getting overwhelmed.

**METRIC**
And measure how much less overwhelmed they think they will be with access to those case studies.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if most students say they will feel less overwhelmed with the help of short case studies from past dual degree students.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. Have you seen a student talk about your specific degree? Do you think if you did it would make course selection less overwhelming?
2.	Do you think having access to case studies side by side from past students will help new dual degree students become less overwhelmed.
3.	What information do you think will be most important from the case studies?
4.	How much less overwhelmed do you think you will be (from a scale of 1 to 10).

## Hypothesis 4: Students will be able to less rely on trial-and-error planning without fear if they have a space to ask questions to past or current students.

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that providing a space to ask questions to past or current students (like a moderated forum or chat) will reduce reliance on trial-and-error planning.

**TEST**
To verify that, we will interview students accordingly and ask them if having a space for questions to previous or current students will reduce reliance on trial-on-error planning.

**METRIC**
And measure how much less reliant on trial-on-error planning accordingly.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if more students would prefer a space to ask questions (like a moderated forum or chat) compared to trial-on-error planning.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1.	Do you think having a space to chat with another student, who is doing the same course or have done the same course in the past will help?
2.	If you could redesign UQs public programs and courses page, would you? How?
3.	What do you find is the most annoying feature of the course profile?
4.	How much less reliant would be you trial-on-error planning after this?
