# Value Proposition Canvas - UQ Student completing a dual degree

## Customer Profile

**The Jobs**
For students undertaking dual degrees at UQ, the main goal is to complete both qualifications on time. This requires careful planning to ensure courses from two programs fit together without clashes. These students need guidance on selecting subjects, sequencing them correctly, and meeting prerequisites for both degrees. Their context is more complex than single-degree students because they must manage two sets of requirements, often across different faculties and majors. Their timetable flexibility is limited, and they rely heavily on advice from peers who have faced the same challenges.

Dual degree students are under greater time pressure than others. Enrolling in the wrong subject can easily cause clashes or delays that affect both programs. A reliable way to enter their details and generate a clear plan would reduce stress and give them confidence that they are progressing correctly in each degree.

**Gains**
Students doing dual degrees value tools that save time and reduce complexity. They expect systems that are accurate, intuitive, and capable of handling both sets of requirements. They want reassurance that they are meeting progression rules, not overlooking prerequisites, and will graduate on schedule.

Socially, they want to appear capable and in control of a demanding workload. Emotionally, they seek relief from stress and confidence in their ability to manage two degrees at once. Their aspiration is to complete both qualifications within the standard timeframe, proving that they can meet the challenge successfully. Success is measured by staying on track, avoiding errors, and maintaining balance in their studies.

**Pains of Customers**
The biggest frustration for dual degree students is reconciling two separate degree structures. Information is often scattered across multiple program pages, and current systems are not designed with dual programs in mind. This creates uncertainty about whether they are meeting both sets of requirements.

Students may worry about falling behind or appearing disorganised compared to their peers. Mistakes such as enrolling in the wrong subject can lead to financial costs and extended study times. Common errors include overlooking prerequisites or misinterpreting faculty rules. Barriers to adopting new tools include doubts about accuracy, difficulty navigating new systems, and scepticism about whether a product can truly simplify dual degree planning.
