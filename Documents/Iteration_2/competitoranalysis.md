# Competitor Analysis

## Unique Value Proposition (UVP)
Our project, **UQ Courses**, is designed to solve the problem of fragmented and unintuitive course planning for UQ students. Right now, students have to use multiple different websites and platforms if they want to find course information, check program rules, plan their degree, and hear about other students’ experiences. This process is time-consuming and frustrating, and often students end up turning to Reddit or friends because the official systems are too confusing.  

We believe our unique value proposition is to provide a centralise platform for UQ students that integrates official course/program data with interactive degree planning tools and peer reviews. Unlike current solutions, our platform would be both easy to use, accurate and student-friendly.

## SWOT Analysis of Key Competitors

### 1. UQ Course Profiles
- **Strengths**: This is the official, authoritative source for UQ courses. It is comprehensive and always updated, which makes it reliable for students who want exact details.  
- **Weaknesses**: It is extremely text-heavy and uses formal academic language. It also does not provide any interactivity or reviews.  
- **Opportunities**: There is a chance to improve the user experience by making this information more visual and digestible.
- **Threats**: If UQ ever modernized the platform and added interactivity, it could overlap with what we are planning to build.  

### 2. UQ Programs & Courses
- **Strengths**: This site is the central authority on program rules, prerequisites, and degree requirements. Students rely on it for accurate degree planning.  
- **Weaknesses**: The interface is static and overwhelming. It provides information but no real tools to plan visually or experiment with choices.  
- **Opportunities**: We could build on top of this by offering a clearer and more visual way to see program progression.  
- **Threats**: Similar to Course Profiles, if UQ updated this platform to be more student-friendly, it would directly compete with our project.  

### 3. UQ SiNet
- **Strengths**: This system is already integrated with UQ enrollment and timetabling, so every student has to use it. It is “official” and unavoidable.  
- **Weaknesses**: It is outdated, slow, and confusing to use. Its planning features are minimal and not intuitive.  
- **Opportunities**: We can fill the gap by providing a better front-end for planning before students actually go to SiNet to enroll.  
- **Threats**: If UQ invests in redesigning SiNet with modern features, the uniqueness of our idea may be reduced.  

### 4. UQ Reddit (/r/UQreddit)
- **Strengths**: Reddit provides honest, real experiences from students. It covers details official sources will never mention, like whether a lecturer is engaging or how heavy the workload feels.  
- **Weaknesses**: The information is scattered, unstructured, and often biased or outdated. Students might struggle to find relevant posts.  
- **Opportunities**: We can formalize these student insights into a structured review system, while keeping them searchable and reliable.  
- **Threats**: Students may continue preferring Reddit because of its candidness and community vibe, even if it is less structured.  

### 5. UniMelb Course Planner (Benchmark)
- **Strengths**: This planner sets the standard for degree visualization. It is intuitive, modern, and clearly valued by UniMelb students.  
- **Weaknesses**: It is locked to UniMelb only, so UQ students cannot benefit from it.  
- **Opportunities**: It demonstrates that students do want a tool like this, and we can adapt the idea specifically for UQ.  
- **Threats**: UQ might eventually copy UniMelb’s approach and build its own version.

## Insights and Strategy

**Insights**
- UQ’s current platforms are fragmented. Students have to jump between multiple systems (Course Profiles, Programs & Courses, SiNet) just to plan their degree.  
- The official systems are accurate but not user-friendly, which leaves students feeling lost.  
- Students already turn to Reddit to fill the gap, which shows a clear demand for peer input and reviews.  
- UniMelb’s planner shows that visual degree planning tools are popular and effective, but UQ does not have anything similar.  

**Strategy**
1. **Integration**: Our platform should combine what’s best about the official systems (accuracy, authority) with what students already use unofficially (reviews and experiences).  
2. **Visualization**: We should provide a planner that makes degree progression easy to see and experiment with. This would immediately add value compared to text-heavy official sources.  
3. **Student-friendly design**: Our interface must prioritize usability and clarity.
4. **Trust + Community**: Position the platform as a trustworthy source by pulling official data but combine it with structured peer reviews. This would make it the first balanced tool that blends both perspectives.
